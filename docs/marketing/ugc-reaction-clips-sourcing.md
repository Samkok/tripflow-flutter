# Sourcing real-human reaction UGC for VoyZa

Research date: 2026-07-25. Companion to `voyza-stepps-playbook.md` — the clip is the hook, the optimizer is the payoff.

## The thing to get straight first

"Viral TikTok reaction clip that's copyright free" is a contradiction. Copyright in a creator's clip vests in that creator automatically the moment they hit record. Public ≠ licensed; a like, a tag, a branded-hashtag entry, or even the creator making the post public conveys **no** commercial license. Statutory damages for willful infringement run to **$150,000 per work** under 17 U.S.C. § 504, plus FTC exposure and separate music-licensing claims for the sound.

So there is no repository of "viral TikToks you can use." What exists — and it's better than you'd expect — is a set of libraries that shoot or license the *same footage type* with rights attached. That's what's below.

### The caution nobody on those landing pages mentions

None of the $19 libraries disclose **model releases**. For a commercial app ad featuring an identifiable face, the release is the document that matters — it's separate from copyright, it's the performer's right of publicity, and "I bought a license from a website" is not a defense if that website never had one. Keep copies of whatever license and release documentation each vendor provides.

Practical read: fine for testing which hook wins. Before you put real ad spend behind a specific face, move that winning beat to a named-creator source (DansUGC custom, or a marketplace below) where the release chain is documented.

## Tier 2 — Free / CC0

### What's genuinely free

Commercial use, no attribution: **[Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/search/videos/user-generated%20content/)**, [Mixkit](https://mixkit.co/free-vertical-videos/), [Coverr](https://coverr.co/), [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/videos/search/vertical/), [Vecteezy](https://www.vecteezy.com/free-videos/surprised-reaction), [Vista Create](https://create.vista.com/videos/ugc/). [Pond5](https://www.pond5.com/) gives away select clips free every week and curates vertical collections.

### Why it fails for the reaction hook — checked, not assumed

I pulled the actual Pexels "user generated content" results. They are **landscape professional stock**: "close-up of a person browsing the internet using a tablet," "man scrolling phone at desk in moody lighting," "woman using a laptop." Studio lighting, deliberate framing, curated settings.

The search term is misleading — on stock sites "user generated" means *footage depicting users*, not *footage created by users*. There is no authentic handheld vertical selfie reaction content in these libraries, because the people who shoot that don't upload to stock sites.

This matters more than it sounds. The entire mechanism of the reaction hook is that it doesn't look bought. A well-lit composed "surprised woman" reads as an ad in under a second, and the hook dies exactly where it's supposed to work.

**Verdict:** excellent and free for destination b-roll, map cutaways, airport/train/street texture. Never use it as the face.

Paid stock ([Storyblocks](https://www.storyblocks.com/video/search/ugc) ~949 UGC clips, [Envato Elements](https://elements.envato.com/stock-video/ugc), iStock, Artgrid, Motion Array) has the identical problem, more expensively.

### YouTube Creative Commons

About **3% of YouTube carries a CC BY license**, which does permit commercial reuse and monetization with credit. Filter under *License and rights ownership → Creative Commons Attribution*.

Two reasons the yield is low: CC BY content skews educational/documentary/public-interest, not reaction content. And the license covers **only the video** — it does not override third-party rights in the music, images, or footage inside it. Reaction clips almost always contain someone else's audio. Treat as a long shot.

### The working recipe (validated 2026-07-25)

Two-stage: `soul_2` for the base frame → `seedance_2_0` image-to-video. Costs per clip: **~0.12 credits** for the frame, **45 credits** for 1080p/5s silent video (22.5 at 720p). Frames are effectively free — generate several, throw most away, only animate the keepers.

**Base-frame prompt skeleton** (each block matters; dropping one shows):

```
[FRAMING]   vertical 9:16 front-facing selfie, handheld, slightly canted,
            camera just off eye level. Head and shoulders — BOTH HANDS
            ENTIRELY OUT OF FRAME.
[CHARACTER] ordinary, plain-looking, explicitly "not a model", asymmetric
            features, unstyled hair
[SKIN]      visible pores, oil sheen on forehead/nose, dark circles, patchy
            stubble or mild redness, no makeup, no filter
[CAMERA]    native iPhone front camera, 26mm equiv, mild barrel softness,
            neural depth blur ONLY at hair edges
[LIGHTING]  available light only, two clashing colour temperatures
            (cool window + warm lamp), asymmetric unfilled shadows
[BACKGROUND] readable, NOT blurred — real clutter: unmade bed, dirty counter,
            car headrest, sagging sofa
[REALITY]   ISO noise 500-900 (800-1200 in low light), sensor grain, lens
            smudge, mild vignette, chromatic aberration at high-contrast edges
[NEGATIVE]  no text, no letters, no logos, no labels, no watermark anywhere
Styling Overlay: raw UGC realism, no cinematic polish, no bloom,
                 true iPhone capture, emphasis on authenticity
```

**Video prompt** = timestamped reaction beats over 5s + identity lock + camera-move ban:

```
"She does NOT speak, no dialogue. Both hands stay completely out of frame.
 One continuous take, no cuts.
 [0.0-1.0s] neutral, scrolling, one natural blink
 [1.0-2.0s] eyes stop and fix, brows draw together — the catch
 [2.0-3.2s] brows jump, eyes widen, silent intake of breath
 [3.2-4.2s] looks up to lens, small disbelieving head shake
 [4.2-5.0s] uneven smile spreads, leans closer to screen
 Throughout: micro-sways, involuntary blinks, breathing in the shoulders.
 Keep the exact same face / clothing / background as the first frame.
 Preserve the ISO grain and mixed colour temperature.
 No dolly, no zoom, no push-in, no colour grading. Raw amateur iPhone footage."
```

**Hard-won gotchas:**

- **Hands are the tell.** Frame them out entirely — not "hands relaxed", *out of frame*. Fingers morph on every model.
- **Ban text in the negative prompt.** Without it you get gibberish pseudo-lettering on clothing tags and fridge doors — an instant giveaway. It still leaks into far-background paper; crop or reroll.
- **Say "not a model" explicitly.** Left alone, the model drifts toward conventionally attractive faces, which reads as an ad. Attractive = bought = hook dies.
- **Keep the base frame emotionally NEUTRAL — put all the reaction in the video prompt.** Baking "sceptical, brow furrowed" into the still cost one whole clip: soul_2 rendered the furrows hard, seedance amplified them, and the man ended up looking like he was wincing at bad news rather than being impressed. A smooth resting face gives the animation room to build the arc. Add explicit negatives to the video prompt too — "never frowns, never winces, never looks distressed; this is a positive reaction from start to finish."
- **Render times vary wildly.** Most 1080p/5s clips land in ~2 minutes; one took over 6. Not stuck — just wait.
- **The API suggests a preset and refuses to run literally.** Pass `declined_preset_id: <the suggested id>` to force your own choreography.
- **Concurrency cap ~3 seedance jobs.** A 4th returns `429 rate_limit_reached`; queue them in batches.
- **Set `generate_audio: false`.** Silent is what you want for a hook you'll caption later, and it costs less.

## What actually makes these hooks work

From a 34,635-clip analysis, the highest-performing hook type is **Product/Outcome Showcase** — the finished result or transformation shown in the first 2 seconds. Not the reaction alone. The reaction buys you the two seconds; the outcome keeps them.

- Ideal hook length: **2–5 seconds / 8–15 words**. The best land under 2s.
- "I wish I knew this sooner" works because it *pre-qualifies* the audience — fewer wrong viewers, higher completion rate, better distribution.
- The 12 styles: Curiosity Gap, Contrarian, Identity Call, Result First, Mid-Action, Number/Stat, Confession, Question, Controversy, Storytelling, Shock Value, Social Proof.
- 2026 skews raw authenticity, specific numbers, confession openings. Polished corporate intros underperform.
- TikTok travel content that wins is shot on iPhone in natural light. Small/unexpected destinations outperform major cities.
- Free research tool: **[TikTok Creative Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/)** — Top Ads library filtered by industry/region/objective, trending sounds and hashtags, no login required for most of it. Use it to see what's live in travel right now rather than guessing.

## VoyZa-specific structure

The playbook says the retellable kernel is the optimizer: *"I dumped 47 saved places in and it gave me 9 perfect days."* That's your video. The bought clip is only the first two seconds.

```
0–2s   borrowed reaction face + spoken hook
2–5s   screen record: the chaotic pin scatter
5–8s   the optimize snap (the emotional peak — this is the product shot)
8–12s  the payoff number ("nine days. eleven seconds.")
12s    CTA
```

Hook lines mapped to the playbook's angles:

1. **Confession + Result First** — "I wish I knew this before I wasted three days in Tokyo."
2. **Number/Stat** — "47 saved places. I thought I had to plan it myself." → snap → "Nine days. Eleven seconds."
3. **Identity Call at the habitat moment** — "POV: flights booked, forty tabs open, zero plan." (The playbook's "flights booked… now what?" window — frequent, weakly claimed, close to the action.)
4. **Curiosity Gap** — "No one told me there's an app that just… does the itinerary."
5. **Practical Value, narrow** — "Three mistakes that waste a whole day in Rome." Narrow beats broad; it summons the one friend planning that exact trip.

Keep the numbers. Per the playbook, numbers are what survive retelling.

Emotion target: awe and excitement, not relief. Sell the extra afternoon in Rome, not "hassle-free planning."

## Recommendation

### Zero-budget path (start here)

1. **Your own face, first.** Solo founder + iPhone + natural light is the highest-performing production setup on travel TikTok in 2026 — not a limitation, the actual optimum. Founder-shot beats bought-stock on this format, and the confession hook ("I built this because I wasted three days in Tokyo") is one only you can deliver honestly.
2. **Higgsfield/Motion credits for the faces you can't shoot.** Already paid for. Use `realistic-ugc-video` for the reaction hooks that need someone who isn't you.
3. **Pexels/Mixkit for destination b-roll only.** Never as the face.
4. **Ship 10 variants** of the same 12-second structure — same body, different first two seconds. The hook is the only variable worth testing early. Organic, no spend.
5. **Build the recipient ask.** Prompt trip-share recipients for a reaction clip. Free, converts best, and it's the supply competitors can't buy.

### When you have $19 to spend

**RE/UGC.** It's the single highest-leverage nineteen dollars here — 182+ real-human hooks organized by emotion *and* consumer-app niche, 7-day refund. Buys you faces and emotional range you can't shoot alone, and lets you test 10 hooks in an afternoon.

### Before you put ad spend behind a winner

Re-shoot the winning beat through **DansUGC custom ($9)** or **JoinBrands ($25–29)** so there's a named creator and a documented model release behind the face in your paid ads. Cheap insurance at the point where the exposure becomes real.
